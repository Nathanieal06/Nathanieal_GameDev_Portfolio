Zatun Zombie Game

Unity 6 URP C#
Role Gameplay & Systems Programmer
Team Size 1 (Solo Developer)
Duration ~1 Week
Engine/Tools Unity 6 (LTS), C#
Platform PC (Windows/macOS/Linux)
Status Completed Demo

My Contribution

As the sole programmer and developer on this technical test, I architected and implemented the entire codebase from scratch using Unity 6.x. Major systems include:

  • Combat & Equipment System: A data-driven modular weapon system built on Scriptable Objects (WeaponBase.cs, EquipmentManager.cs). It handles distinct weapon types (Pistol, Rifle, Knife) using a hybrid hitscan-projectile algorithm for perfect hit registration. Architecture relies on C# Events (GameEvents.cs) to decouple combat logic from UI updates.
  • AI & Enemy Director: NavMesh-based AI (ZombieController.cs, ZombieSpawner.cs) capable of obstacle avoidance and tracking the player. It uses a State-machine approach with Coroutines for throttled decision-making and spawning, drastically reducing CPU overhead in scenes with 100+ enemies.
  • Procedural Animation Rigging: Handled third-person movement states (walk/sprint/ADS) intertwined with Unity Animation Rigging. Implemented procedural Aim IK, head tracking, and Multi-Parent Constraints to maintain hand-to-weapon consistency dynamically.
  • Dynamic Camera System: Cinemachine-based state-driven camera (CameraSwitcher.cs) for seamless, weighted transitions between Hip-Fire and Aim-Down-Sights (ADS) field of view and offset configurations.

Technical Deep Dive

1. Hybrid Hitscan-Projectile System

The Problem: Fast physical projectiles (Rigidbodies) in Unity often suffer from "tunneling" at high framerates, while purely instant hitscans lack satisfying visual feedback.

The Approach: Separated the physics logic from the visual rendering. A hitscan raycast evaluates hit registration and applies damage instantly and 100% reliably. Concurrently, a "Visual Only" physical bullet prefab is launched purely for the player's visual feedback.

Why it's Notable: Delivers the visual immersion of physical ballistics with the zero-latency, competitive-grade reliability of a hitscan weapon.

[Header("Mode")]
[Tooltip("Enable this only when a separate hitscan ray should handle all damage.")]
[SerializeField] private bool visualOnly = false;

private void Start() {
    // Ignore bullet-to-player layer collisions globally at the engine level. 
    // Bullets never hit the player's own colliders regardless of spawn offset.
    Physics.IgnoreLayerCollision(bulletLayer, playerLayer, true);
}

2. SRP Batcher Compatible Dissolve Shader

The Problem: Standard dissolve effects on dozens of dying zombies break Unity's batching, leading to an explosion of draw calls and CPU bottlenecks.

The Approach: Hand-wrote a custom HLSL URP shader (URP_Dissolve_Texture.shader) strictly utilizing CBUFFER_START(UnityPerMaterial). Used clip() instead of alpha transparency for Early-Z discard optimization.

Why it's Notable: Maintained full compatibility with the SRP Batcher, allowing Unity to draw dozens of zombies at different dissolve stages in a single batched call.

3. Global AI & Physics Throttling

The Problem: Running distance checks, Line of Sight, and NavMesh calculations for large swarms of zombies every frame in Update() causes massive CPU spikes.

The Approach: Distributed heavy calculations across multiple frames using Coroutines. Additionally, implemented a static global flag IsCutsceneActive that instantly suspends all AI logic globally during menus.

Why it's Notable: Highly scalable architecture that ensures a stable 60+ FPS on mid-range hardware even with over 100 active enemies.

Automation Tools Pipeline

To streamline development workflows and resolve asset inconsistencies, I developed custom Python utility scripts to automate batch processing:

  • fix_all_prefabs.py: Automates the batch processing of weapon prefabs and asset metadata. It resets prefab settings, validates component hierarchies, and updates metadata references instantly.
  • fix_muzzleflash.py: Automatically corrects the alignment and scaling of muzzle flash VFX across all weapon variants to ensure visual consistency without manual tweaking.
  • fix_prefabs.py: A general utility that crawls through the asset database to repair and validate prefab data consistency.

Postmortem / Lessons Learned

  • AI Refactoring & Optimization: Basic distance checks require looping through all targets, which scales poorly with 100+ enemies. Using physics-based spatial partitioning (like Physics.OverlapSphereNonAlloc) allows the engine's optimized C++ backend to handle proximity queries natively. In future projects, I will architect AI perception around spatial hashes or Unity's Job System to decouple logic from the main thread.
  • Editor Tooling & Asset Pipelines: The necessity for a batch-repair workflow arose because third-party weapon assets had inconsistent pivot points and missing colliders. To avoid this technical debt, I will establish strict import guidelines and utilize Unity's AssetPostprocessor to automatically enforce structural templates on import.
  • Modular Weapon Refactor: The shift from a centralized weapon controller to a modular prefab-based design was driven by the need to scale. A centralized controller becomes a bottleneck as weapon complexity grows. Taking the time to decouple the logic paid massive dividends, as adding the rifle and knife took minutes instead of hours.
  • Addressing Stability (NullRefs): The NullReferenceException issues were a result of the decoupled Event-driven architecture firing callbacks before dependent UI components were fully initialized in Awake(). In my next project, I will implement a robust Dependency Injection framework (like VContainer) to strictly manage initialization order.
